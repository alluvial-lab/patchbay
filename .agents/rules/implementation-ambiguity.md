# Implementation Ambiguity Surfacing

Agile-workflow's autopilot caller note tells delegated implementers to "resolve ambiguities with judgment, log rationale in the item body, and do not ask strategic questions unless a hard halt condition applies." This harness does not provide a structured question tool, so interactive prompts get swallowed. The rule below refines which ambiguities may be resolved in-stride and which must surface as blockers.

- **Mechanical / syntactic ambiguities may be resolved in-stride with judgment.** Examples: a language syntax question with a verifiable answer (Quint map-literal form, `pure def` vs `def`, operator availability, tool-flag quirks), a test-harness detail, a naming choice with no semantic consequence. Log these under `## Implementation notes` (the seed arc's "Implementation discovery" sections are the model). This is not swallowing — it is implementation.
- **Semantic 50/50 ambiguities must surface as a blocker, not be resolved with judgment.** A semantic 50/50 is a choice between two defensible options where the decision affects protocol semantics, a safety claim, a state-machine shape, an authority boundary, or a reserved-seam disposition — and the design / story spec does not already pin it. Append a `## Blocker` section to the item body naming the question, the options, and why it is not mechanical; commit; stop. Do NOT resolve it with judgment and do NOT log it as a `## Design decisions` entry. The operator is the alignment signal.
- **The test: "would a different reasonable implementer pick a different option and produce a materially different model?"** If yes, it is a semantic 50/50 — surface it. If no (only one option typechecks / passes the checker / matches the spec), it is mechanical — resolve it.
- **When in doubt, surface.** The cost of blocking and asking is low; the cost of a silent semantic commitment in a safety-claiming formal model is high. The seed arc's review history (6 self-defining properties caught only at review) is the evidence that silent commitments are the failure mode this verification program exists to prevent.
- **Under autopilot, a blocker on a sequential depends_on chain stops the run.** Report the blocker up to the orchestrator, which surfaces it to the operator. Do not continue to the next story.
